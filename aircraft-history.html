Aircraft History

The Monster Cub was not created as a completely new aircraft — it evolved from the proven Piper PA-18 Super Cub, a legendary tailwheel aircraft introduced in the late 1940s. What followed was a long history of modifications and upgrades that transformed the Super Cub into the powerful, rugged aircraft commonly known as the Monster Cub.

ORIGIN

Built from a proven bush-plane design.

By the late 1940s, Piper had developed the PA-18 Super Cub as an improved successor to the earlier Piper Cub. Instead of starting from scratch, Piper refined the successful Cub formula with a stronger structure, improved aerodynamics, and more powerful engines. Decades later, heavily modified Super Cubs became known as “Monster Cubs,” combining the original aircraft’s rugged design with increased engine power and STOL capability.

1955

First flight

The prototype flies for the first time on 12 June 1955, combining the 170B's wing and fuselage with a new tricycle undercarriage and squared vertical tail.

1956

Production begins

Deliveries of the Cessna 172 start, priced to compete directly with the Piper Tri-Pacer as an easier-to-land trainer for the post-war flying boom.

1960

Skyhawk name introduced

Cessna adds the "Skyhawk" trim package — better soundproofing, upholstery, and instrumentation — which eventually becomes the model's common name.

1963

Swept tail restyle

The straight vertical fin is replaced with the swept, "Omni-Vision" rear-window fuselage still recognizable on the aircraft today.

1986

Production halted

Rising product-liability insurance costs make new piston singles unprofitable across the U.S. industry; Cessna suspends 172 production after roughly 35,000 built.

1994

GARA passed

The General Aviation Revitalization Act caps manufacturer liability at 18 years, removing the main obstacle that had kept piston singles out of production.

1996

Production resumes

Cessna restarts the line as the 172R, now fuel-injected and built in a new Independence, Kansas facility.

2005

Glass cockpit standard

The 172S Skyhawk SP adopts the Garmin G1000 integrated flight deck as standard equipment, replacing the round-dial panel on new-build aircraft.

Today

Still in production

With more than 44,000 built across seven decades, the 172 remains the best-selling aircraft in history and the backbone of flight training worldwide.
